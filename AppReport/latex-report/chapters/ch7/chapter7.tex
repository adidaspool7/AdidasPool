\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{cap:conclusions}

This chapter closes the report. It restates what the project contributed,
checks each objective against what was delivered, distils the lessons
learned --- including the experience of acting as Product Owner of the
two-person talent-pool subproject --- and sets out the limitations and the
future work that follow from them.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:concl-summary}

The work delivered the four contributions claimed in
Chapter~\ref{cap:introduction}, each backed by a concrete deliverable:

\begin{itemize}
	\item A \textbf{reference architecture} for an AI-assisted recruitment
	platform that respects \gls{gdpr} retention while preserving analytical
	value --- realised as the onion-structured Next.js application described
	in Chapter~\ref{cap:design} and deployed to production.
	\item A \textbf{reproducible, auditable job-anchored fit algorithm}
	(\texttt{computeJobFit}) implemented as a pure function with the most
	extensive test coverage in the suite (Chapter~\ref{cap:validation}),
	chosen over opaque embedding ranking precisely because every criterion
	is explainable.
	\item A \textbf{dual-mode AI interviewer} (technical-skill validation
	and CEFR language assessment) integrated through a hardened HMAC token
	contract --- the integration layer being the author's contribution, the
	conversational service the second member's.
	\item A \textbf{project-management experience report} from the
	two-person talent-pool subproject of the BlendEd program, with the author
	as its Product Owner, documented in
	Chapter~\ref{cap:analysis} and reflected on below.
\end{itemize}

\section{Goals Achieved}
\label{sec:concl-goals}

Measured against the seven objectives of Section~\ref{sec:objectives}, the
outcome is as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Centralise candidate data.} \emph{Achieved} --- a
	relational, deletion-aware talent pool replaces the spreadsheets
	(Chapter~\ref{cap:design}).
	\item \textbf{Automate CV ingestion.} \emph{Achieved} --- the
	LLM parsing pipeline with provider fallback extracts structured
	experience, education, languages, skills and field-of-work tags
	(Chapter~\ref{cap:implementation}).
	\item \textbf{Provide job-anchored matching.} \emph{Achieved} --- the
	rule-based, per-criterion \texttt{computeJobFit} engine
	(Chapters~\ref{cap:design} and~\ref{cap:validation}).
	\item \textbf{Verify language proficiency.} \emph{Achieved} --- the
	dual-mode AI interview returns a CEFR level with grammar, vocabulary
	and fluency sub-scores.
	\item \textbf{Empower HR communication.} \emph{Achieved} --- the
	notification and campaign system with a full per-candidate interaction
	history.
	\item \textbf{Make the pipeline measurable.} \emph{Achieved} --- the
	built-in dashboards and the constrained custom-widget builder.
	\item \textbf{Respect compliance and security.} \emph{Largely
	achieved} --- server-side authorisation, a deletion-aware model and
	self-service account erasure are in place; automating the time-based
	retention purge remains future work
	(Section~\ref{sec:concl-future}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Lessons Learned}
\label{sec:concl-lessons}

\subsection{Product Owner Lessons in a Two-Person Subproject}
\label{sec:concl-po}

Holding the Product Owner accountability while also writing most of the code
was the defining experience of the talent-pool subproject, and its lessons
cut both ways.
The advantage was immediacy: there was no translation loss between deciding
what mattered and building it. The danger was the mirror image of that
advantage --- when the same person sets priorities and implements them, the
backlog can quietly bend toward what is interesting to build rather than what
the client needs. Two practices kept this in check. First, the backlog was
disciplined to be \emph{client-driven by construction}: every item traced to
a validated pain point, and MoSCoW prioritisation
(Section~\ref{sec:method-backlog}) made deferral an explicit, defensible
decision rather than a silent omission. Second, the fusion of sprint review
with client acceptance meant that the people who owned the problem signed off
each increment, which is the closest a one-person product role can get to an
external check. Within the team, the sprint review was also the moment the
second developer could push back on product decisions, since the increment
was concrete and demonstrable rather than abstract. The honest takeaway is
that combining the roles is workable at this scale only with deliberate
guardrails against scope creep; it is not a structure to carry into a larger
team unchanged.

\subsection{Architectural Lessons}
\label{sec:concl-arch}

The onion architecture justified its overhead at one specific moment: when
the AI Interviewer had to live in a separate Python process. Because the
Domain held no external dependencies and use cases never reached into
infrastructure, extracting the interviewer behind a typed token contract was
a boundary change, not a rewrite. The counter-lesson concerns the decision
to forgo an ORM: the manual \texttt{camelize}/\texttt{snakeify} conversion
between database and domain was a real, recurring cost, and the May~2026
audit showed it was also where type safety leaked (rows cast through
\texttt{any}). The cost was bounded and the strict compiler largely filled
the gap, but the experience is a reminder that removing a layer of
abstraction moves work elsewhere rather than eliminating it.

\subsection{AI Lessons}
\label{sec:concl-ai}

Three lessons recurred whenever a Large Language Model touched persistent
data. First, \textbf{structured-output prompting beats free-form} for any
value that must land in a column: validating model output against a strict
Zod schema, and tolerantly discarding invented values, was the difference
between trustworthy and unusable parsing. Second, \textbf{LLM output schemas
must be versioned}, because the shape the model is asked to produce evolves;
storing a schema version alongside parsed requirements let the system
re-parse only what was stale. Third, \textbf{fallback providers are a
reliability mechanism, not a luxury} --- the Groq-to-OpenAI fallback turned a
single point of failure into a degradation rather than an outage.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:concl-limitations}

The work has clear limits. The validation survey drew on a small sample from
a single organisation, so its results are directional rather than
statistically generalisable. Automated test coverage, though substantial, is
concentrated in the Domain and Application layers and is thinner over some
use-case orchestration and the thin infrastructure adapters. The spoken
assessment relies on browser-native speech recognition and synthesis, which
restricts it to Chromium-based browsers. CV ingestion extracts text directly
from the uploaded file and has no optical-character-recognition (OCR)
fallback, so scanned or image-only PDFs yield too little text to parse and are
flagged for manual review rather than processed automatically. Bulk imports
are processed serially in a time-bounded background task, which keeps a single
run to roughly 150--180 CVs and means the 500-file batch cap cannot always
complete in one window without a durable queue. And although candidates can
erase their own account on demand, the time-based retention purge is not yet
automated --- it still depends on a manual trigger rather than running on a
schedule.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl-future}

The most immediate future work is the tracked backlog from the May~2026
audit: extracting the remaining interview routes behind a use-case layer,
replacing residual \texttt{any} casts at the repository boundary with typed
row interfaces, and pre-filtering candidates in SQL before the matcher to
scale matching on large pools. Beyond the audit, several directions stand out:
automating the time-based retention purge as a scheduled, GDPR-compliant job;
widening
the analytics widget catalogue so analysts can express more questions safely;
adding an offline transcription path (for example a Whisper-based fallback)
so the spoken assessment works on browsers without the Web Speech API; adding
an OCR fallback to the ingestion pipeline so scanned and image-only CVs can be
parsed automatically; moving bulk parsing onto a durable queue with a
dedicated worker so large imports are no longer bounded by a single background
window; enabling accent-insensitive deduplication through the PostgreSQL
\texttt{unaccent} extension; and
introducing organisation-level data isolation to make the platform
multi-tenant.

\section{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:concl-closing}

The project set out to help a small HR team manage a structured pool of
candidates efficiently, compliantly and usefully, under the constraint of
GDPR retention. It delivered a deployed platform that automates CV ingestion,
matches candidates to specific jobs in an explainable way, verifies language
proficiency through an AI interview, and gives recruiters the communication
and analytics tools the original pain points called for. Just as valuable as
the software, for the author, was the experience of carrying a product from
client discovery through architecture, implementation and validation while
acting as the subproject's Product Owner --- a rehearsal, at a manageable
scale, of how
technical and product responsibility combine to turn a real organisational
problem into working software.
