\begin{abstract}

adidas Global Business Services (GBS) Porto employs more than 900 people ---
around 22\% of them international --- and operates daily across 15 business
languages. In such an environment language capability is a strategic asset,
yet it is hard to assess objectively, inconsistently tracked across
recruitment cycles, and rarely developed in a structured way after hiring.
The client brief that motivated this project asked for a sustainable framework
to \emph{attract}, \emph{develop} and \emph{retain} those capabilities in
alignment with the company's long-term business objectives and cultural
diversity. This strategic goal is compounded by an operational constraint:
the General Data Protection Regulation forces the deletion of candidate data
after six months, yet recruiters still need to recognise returning
candidates, re-engage promising profiles, and screen large CV batches under
tight deadlines. This report describes the design and implementation of
\emph{TalentHub}, an integrated HR platform for candidate lifecycle
management developed during the BlendEd program in partnership with adidas
GBS Porto: a web application that operationalises that framework by
combining a structured talent pool, automated CV parsing, job-anchored
candidate matching, an HR communication and campaign layer, and an
AI-driven language and skill assessment module.

The work documented here covers the full lifecycle of the project: client
discovery and pain-point analysis, requirements engineering, architectural
design following onion / clean principles, database modelling on Supabase
PostgreSQL, integration of two Large Language Models (Groq Llama~3.3 70B as
primary and OpenAI GPT-4o as fallback) for CV parsing, job-description
extraction and conversational interviewing, and the deployment of a
production-grade Next.js application on Vercel. TalentHub was the Talent Pool
subproject of the BlendEd program --- one of four sub-teams within a single
group serving a shared client, adidas; it was delivered by a two-person
engineering team under the program's Scrum process, with the author acting as
the subproject's Product Owner and implementing every module except the AI
Interviewer FastAPI sidecar (developed by the second team member). Validation
combines automated testing
(527 unit tests across 33 files at the time of writing), a Supabase security
advisor sweep, and a structured client-feedback survey informed by nine
documented pain points.

The resulting platform replaces ad-hoc spreadsheets with a structured,
analytics-ready talent pool, makes CV-derived data reusable across openings,
and introduces a reproducible CEFR-based language verification flow. The project also
yielded a reusable architectural reference for AI-assisted recruitment tools
that respect GDPR retention constraints.

\textbf{Keywords (Theme):} Recruitment, Talent Pool, Human Resources Tooling,
GDPR, Language Assessment.

\textbf{Keywords (Technologies):} Next.js, TypeScript, Supabase, Large
Language Models, Structured-Output Prompting, FastAPI.

\end{abstract}
